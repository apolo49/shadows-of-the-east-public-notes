# Leaving Pithertara

*[[Setting/Semantics/Dating/index|5th Hanomi, 1782 IOG]]*

[[Rhoda of the Shimmering Moontide - Player Notes|Rhoda]] and [[Evangeline Marquis|Yvaquis]] were the first out the door, well before the rest of the inn had properly stirred, making for the animal stables where [[Kitty|the black cat]] and [[The Black Hare]] were kept. Amid the manure and hay and wheat and every other foul, fragrant thing a stable accumulates, the two of them settled in to meditate — herbs already burning between them, well before breakfast.

[[John Ironchest - Player Notes|John Ironchest]] left not long after, [[Ulriki]] in tow, off to secure the boy's travel papers for the long road to [[Duchy of Brackenfall|Brackenfall]].

Back upstairs, [[Riley - Player Notes|Riley]] woke to find himself frozen to his own breakfast.

"How has my life — my *entire* life — amounted to being frozen to food in the morning?" he muttered, blinking against the cold. "Why is my tail cold? Why is my tail *frozen*?" He turned a frightened glare on [[Ellowen Aurelian Vale|Ellowen]], already awake and hunched over her journal, illustrating the previous day's events with no idea anything was wrong. "Ellowen. Help."

She looked up, and her eyes finally landed on him. "Oh my gods! Your face is frozen to my desk!" Her first concern, visibly, was for the desk. Bewildered as she was, she couldn't help but be a little fascinated too.

"Help me," Riley pleaded again. "I am cold."

Ellowen combed frantically through her memory and her spellbook, the two of them united, for once, in a single shared goal: not turning Riley into a pile of smouldering ash on the furniture. Riley, because it was his life. Ellowen, because she did not want blood on her hands — especially not a new friend's.

From the bed, [[Big Beefy Bob - Player Notes|Big Beefy Bob]] woke entirely oblivious to the unfolding crisis, already coaxing [[Pupolas Crate]] up to go find some gruel downstairs. On his way to the door he paused to offer, with total sincerity, "I will get you some gruel too, bestie! Another bowl of gruel!"

"I love gruel..." Ellowen said aloud, lying through her teeth, lamenting the very thought of something so foul entering her noble Brackenfallian body.

Then the solution struck her — one that left her blameless for whatever came next. "Can you use [[Elements#Fire Stone|your rock]]?" she proposed, brightening.

Riley wasn't sure, but he saw no better option. He reached into his cloak, pulled out his fire stone, and pressed it to his frozen cheek. The heat singed him — not badly, but enough to leave a small burn — and thawed the ice enough to free him. The cheese, meanwhile, melted straight onto the table, and in the process Riley accidentally invented the [[Rileywich#Grilled Rileywich|Grilled Cheese Rileywich]], a variation on the standard [[Rileywich]] he'd created some time before. He turned to Ellowen. "Thanks, Ellowen," he said, dripping sarcasm.

"I never told you to burn yourself," she shot back. "I said use your rock. It's not my fault you're not intelligent enough to think your actions through."

Out at the stables, Rhoda and Yvaquis — thoroughly high by now — turned their attention to the Black Hare. Rhoda tried questioning the horse directly, curious whether there was something wrong with its owner, but got nothing back for her trouble. Yvaquis, meanwhile, convinced she'd spotted something in the hay, began tearing through the straw with increasing urgency, flinging it in every direction and finding absolutely nothing. She moved on to the water troughs, wading her hands through them with the same fruitless result.

"Yvaquis, stop it!" Rhoda snapped. "I am trying to find out whether John is nasty and hurts the horse." She looked the Black Hare over properly and concluded, at least, that the animal seemed content — though some of its muscle looked oddly overgrown, shifted into place as if by some outside force. Yvaquis wondered aloud if the horse might simply be magic, and reached for [[Elements#Earth Stone|her own stone]] to check. Nothing. She tried asking the horse questions again. Silence again. A last search through the stables for gold turned up nothing either, and disappointed and hungry, she sat down and smoked the last of her herbs, sharing what remained with Rhoda.

John and Ulriki reached the keep. At the gate, his path was blocked and his business questioned.

"Papers; for the boy," he said, and was waved through.

The courtyard beyond was pebbled and vast, practically a village unto itself — smith, butcher, storage shed, armoury, noble stables, everything a settlement could need, packed inside a few walls around an irregular square. To his right stood a large stone building, unmistakably the one he wanted. Inside, he found a hall dressed in gold and silver, candelabras hanging from chained fixtures, banners and gonfalons lining the walls beside Doric orders — the kind of place built a couple hundred years back, in an age of real prosperity.

An administrator asked his business. "I need papers for this boy to travel to Brackenfall," John said, gesturing at Ulriki.

The man dug through his ledgers, read them over, and delivered the verdict calmly. "That's going to cost you about fifty gold. It's quite expensive, especially to a place like Brackenfall — even more so with the diplomatic situation going on. After all, getting him there is now our guarantee, and if anything happens, it's on us, on behalf of the Emperor, to guarantee his passage. Not just on the way there — while he's there, too."

The first part of that sentence went entirely over John's head. "What diplomatic situation?"

"Someone went missing. The lady of the duchy. She was taken from [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Amphos|Amphos]] — *the city*, *our capital* — while under *our* protection." He shook his head. "[[Alaric Aurelian Vale - Player Notes|Her father]] has reported her missing, and it's quite the important problem, as she was recently betrothed to [[The Prince - Player Notes|the prince]]."

John turned that over, and felt the weight of it — and also the plainer problem that he couldn't spare fifty gold for Ulriki's sake. Then an idea struck him.

"I can't part with fifty gold, but..." He produced his military border-guard badge, earned at the [[Empire of Solara|Solari]] northern border with [[Duchy of Dornsia|Dornsia]]. "Would this suffice?"

The administrator studied it. "For a veteran, I can do twenty gold." He could see John's hesitation and pressed on. "I can guarantee safe passage with this note — this pass-port, as we call it. Any problems the duchy causes will mean major diplomatic repercussions for Brackenfall, if anything happens to him on their territory."

That was enough to settle it. "Do you know of any good caravans?" John asked.

"If he waits a couple of days, there'll be a caravan running through here to Brackenfall."

John paid the twenty gold, handed Ulriki fifty silver — five gold — for lodging, food, and passage once the caravan arrived, and repeated everything he'd learned to the boy, word for word. Then he went further: he parted with his shield spell scroll and a hand-written workout regimen meant to help Ulriki build muscle and grow stronger. Their goodbye was a sad one. "Live strong," John told him, "and live long." Ulriki walked off toward the Iron Chest, sad but visibly excited, his eyes twinkling with something, maybe a hint of sadness. John watched him go before turning north, toward the farmhouses, in search of grain.

Downstairs at the inn, Bob approached the counter with more enthusiasm than eloquence. "Can I get some gruel please? Enough for me, my bestie, my dog, maybe the cat?"

"Three or four bowls?" the bartender asked, exasperated but game.

Bob considered this seriously, looking at his hands as though the answer might be written there. "Are they big bowls?"

"Probably about the size of your palm."

Bob studied first one palm, then the other, then back again, unsure whether it mattered which. His palms were pretty big, after all. "Yeah, many bowls please!"

"Three or four?" the innkeeper tried again.

"Um. Yes."

"Four it is!" the innkeeper declared, done negotiating. "Two silver then, please."

Bob dug out his coin-purse and began counting — well past two, with no clear sense of when to stop — before landing, by luck, on exactly two silver, which he slammed triumphantly onto the counter. "There you go! Thank you!" Both declarations arrived at a volume better suited to a battlefield.

The innkeeper filled four wooden bowls, each smaller than Bob's palm but not by much, and set them down. "How are you to take these?"

"My hand?" Bob said, baffled that this needed asking.

Eager to move him along, the innkeeper stacked two bowls on each of Bob's palms, gruel smearing between them into a small leaning tower on either hand. Bob was delighted with the arrangement. "C'mon, Pupolas Crate," he said, and made his way back upstairs.

He returned to find Riley up on the desk, a fresh burn on his cheek, hissing and jerking while Ellowen tried to tend to it.

"What up, bestie!" Bob announced himself, missing the tone of the room entirely. He handed out bowls regardless — one to Ellowen ("Here you go, bestie! One for you!"), one to Riley ("Got you one, cat!"), and one to the floor for Pupolas ("And you too, dog"), before settling onto the bed to dig into his own.

Ellowen, disgusted by the sight of gruel again and now smeared across her workspace besides, cast Prestidigitation over the mess. It vanished as if a maid had come through — and, as an unintended side effect, turned every bowl of gruel in the room to chocolate.

"Chocolate gruel..." Riley lamented. "I'd rather have a grilled cheese Rileywich!"

Ellowen's curiosity got the better of her. "Riley, how did your face happen to be stuck to the table with ice?"

"What? Why are you asking *me*?"

"I don't know!" she shouted back.

"It must've just been cold," Riley offered, sounding no more convinced than she was.

"On your face?!"

"Yes!" he insisted, equally unconvinced. He wasn't stupid, and he wasn't gullible, but he had no answer to give — only the certainty that something was going on. "I don't have magic! I can't just form ice from my cheek to my sandwich to the table. And I definitely can't do it from my *tail* to the table either!"

"It must be Bob! Bob is magic!" Ellowen declared, and Riley — remembering the day in [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Aklebe|Aklebe]] when a misfired Mage Hand sent Bob flying toward the Panting Shadow — found himself agreeing readily. "Bob, was it you?" she asked.

"I am magic, bestie! It must have been me! Oh my! I am so sorry, cat-lady!" Bob apologised at once, just as convinced as they were, and with about as much evidence.

"I was just trying to eat my cheese and my bread! Then it got cold and I fainted! I woke up stuck to the table! And now my face is burnt! I'm going downstairs and I'm getting pissed! See ya!" Riley hopped off the table, startling Ellowen mid-treatment, and stormed out — only to burst back in a second later. "And I'm taking my gruel with me! I'm hungry!" He grabbed the bowl and slammed the door a second time. Ellowen turned to Bob, who hadn't noticed any of it, too busy trying — and failing — to summon magic from his own fingertips.

Downstairs, Riley discovered his gruel no longer tasted of chocolate, now that he'd carried it far enough from the spell's source — bland and stodgy instead. Even so, a wave of nostalgia crept over him: a half-remembered scene from his old lodging, a wood shack, a straw floor, a battered old cloth doing service as a table-runner, a lukewarm bowl of mystery grain. Nothing about it had ever been nice. It had still been home — a home gone now. As a boy he'd complained about the food and eaten it anyway, grateful just to stop being hungry. He couldn't remember [[Wrensu]] ever eating on those days. Maybe the goliath simply hadn't been hungry. Maybe he'd just been doing his duty. "Ah," Riley murmured to himself, lifting his spoon. "Just like Wrensu used to make." He ate it all the same.

Back in the room, Ellowen and Bob talked over what remained of breakfast.

"Riley seems to be having a great day today," Ellowen said, dry as bone.

Sarcasm had never once landed on Bob and never would. "Yeah, lucky cat-lady!" he agreed sincerely. "He's having such a good day! He had my magic; he had my gruel—"

"You did glue his face to the table, though, Bob."

"Yeah, I gotta work on my magic first though."

"I think you should work on your magic. I feel like it could be very dangerous, Bob."

"Yeah! I'm just so magic! It's just so hard being so magic, bestie!"

"You must become my teacher!"

"I should! I should! Bob has so much magic, bestie! I could give you all the magic! I could give you all the tips! We could both be so magic!" The words tumbled out of him at speed.

"I think you need to give me some tips as to how you do your magic," Ellowen said, playing along.

"You just gotta be really big and beefy," Bob said, with the confidence of a man who'd done real research. "That's what I've gathered so far. You gotta be really big, really beefy."

Ellowen looked herself over — small, slender, standard for a half-elf — and felt a flicker of disappointment, though not much of one; the finest mages and scholars of Brackenfall had all been slender too, except the grand mage's advisor, who was larger but no better at the craft, whatever he knew of the theory. "I feel like we have to work on that, Bob..." she admitted.

"Mm. Have some more gruel. Have some more gruel!" Bob said, shovelling some of his own bowl over to supplement her diet, entirely earnest in his generosity.

"Mmm! Chocolate gruel!"

Rhoda and Yvaquis wandered in downstairs to find Riley eating alone, nostalgic, burnt, and sullen. Rhoda danced over and struck up a tune — perfectly inoffensive, competent enough, the kind of performance nobody claps for. Riley didn't look up. He kept eating, determinedly ignoring her, while Yvaquis egged the performance on and Rhoda, oblivious, grew more convinced everyone adored her playing.

Riley slipped away toward the bar, sarcastically feigning enjoyment as he went, and asked for a full bottle of rum. He was out, and he needed it. Yvaquis caught the shift. "Uh, Riley—"

"I'm shopping!" he snapped.

"Can we have some, please? Y'know — sharing," she pressed anyway.

"I was about to say the same," Rhoda added.

Riley wasn't having it, in principle or in practice. "No! There's only one person I share with — and *definitely* not alcohol — and they're not here right now!"

Yvaquis turned to Rhoda instead. "Would you like a drink? What would you like?"

"Yes please — the strongest they have."

Riley finished his own purchase — a gallon of rum for seven electrum — and Yvaquis and Rhoda, having overheard the price, hatched a plan for a discount morning drink of their own. Rhoda started up an erratic, deliberately terrible drumbeat, hoping to distract him; Riley simply turned his nose up and ignored it. Yvaquis, improvising, threw herself to the floor beside him screaming that she'd broken her leg, winking frantically at Rhoda to signal it was fake — a wink Rhoda entirely missed. Riley simply stepped over her. The other patrons tutted at the pair like they were disruptive lunatics. Yvaquis got back up without difficulty. "Screw you!" she told Riley, then pivoted straight into flattery.

"Riley..."

"What?" he said, uninterested after the failed act.

"Riley, my leg is really painful right now—"

"No it's not."

"It really is."

"I can make it painful for real."

"Honestly," she tried once more, then abandoned the lie entirely. "You're looking really nice today actually, Riley."

"I was thinking that too, actually," Rhoda chimed in.

"You feeling good?" Yvaquis pushed.

"No."

"Your burn makes you look really cool," she tried. "You look tough."

"I'm leaving."

"I love your outfit, is something different?"

"I'm going," he said, standing.

"Going where?"

"Anywhere." He left through the door despite their protests, Rhoda even throwing herself against it in a last attempt to block him. He slipped past anyway.

"I need some alcohol to lubricate my throat," Rhoda tried one final time.

"D'ya? Then go ask at the bar!" Riley called back, hood up, in his lightest girl-voice. "*Byeeee!*"

Rhoda and Yvaquis looked at each other. "We're not very good at this," Rhoda admitted. Giving up on Riley entirely, they went to the bar themselves and bought two mugs of rum for five silver each, plus a loaf of garlic bread for one silver.

John reached the farm to find a field of nothing — hard, wet, cold ground, harvested some months back, in, Atmi, the tenth month of the Solari Calendar. It is winter, but it is still a sorry sight to see. To the north stood a house with a small silo, which he guessed might hold grain. A tired but hopeful-looking farmer appeared around the side of his house.

"Do you have some grain?" John asked.

"Well, yes, we have some," the man said, wary. "How much do you need?"

"How much do you have?"

"We have fifty barrels worth, but they have to last a long time. The empire is vast, but too vast to get anything here worthwhile."

"I have a three-day trip coming up to [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Myrneleu|Myrneleu]] — do you know anything about it?"

"I know bits and bobs, not much. Never been myself. Heard whispers from travellers at the inn — it's just that place some ships go through, toward Itrea, [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Dikon|Dikon]], and other places in [[Itria Province]]."

"Will it have any resources for us?"

"It's a common pilgrimage route. I'm certain it will."

"Thank you for your information and co-operation," John said. "If you can spare me ten barrels, and a barrel of apples, that would be most appreciated."

The farmer worked the sums aloud. "A barrel of grain, this time of year, this short supply — that's a gold each, so ten of those is ten gold. A barrel of apples is two gold. Twelve gold together, please."

John found this steep, and the farmer, watching him weigh his coin-purse nervously in his hand, tried to justify it. "I also have to keep some for the local populace, remember — it's more than just this city, sometimes the surrounding villages too!"

"Do you have any manual labour available?" John asked. "Maybe some quid-pro-quo?"

"My drain is clogged, for the house. Maybe you can help with that? I may be able to knock some gold off if you help me with my drain problem."

John wound up and threw a punch to the side, the compressed air ripping through the guttering and clearing the drain instantly — and, in the process, tearing several roof tiles loose, which shattered against the ground.

"Ah — I have unclogged your drain," John said, "and also exposed a flaw in your roof. Maybe you should switch tilers while you're at it."

The farmer's face fell. John, masking his embarrassment as best he could, cast Minor Illusion to make it look as though he'd repaired the tiles at incredible speed.

"Wow! How did you fix my roof that quick? You literally just destroyed it!" the farmer marvelled, thoroughly impressed. "Nine gold! Take whatever you need!"

"I'm John Ironchest. That's how I did it," John said, and took what he'd asked for. Eyeing the sheer bulk of it, he asked if he could borrow a wagon to haul it all to the Zoraneset. The farmer agreed, for two extra silver, and John pulled the cart like a rickshaw and sprinted for the ship, determined to make it back within the hour to fix the roof properly.

Riley, having left the tavern where Yvaquis and Rhoda had tried to relieve him of his rum, was wandering the square in a foul mood. *Who dares to steal rum from those unfortunate,* he thought bitterly. *It's one thing to give to the poor from the rich, but to take from the poor... they could've just bought some at the bar themselves. I know they have enough money.*

He scanned the crowd — perhaps a hundred people, two hundred pockets, most likely carrying coin. But there had to be a *reason* behind his actions; he couldn't rob people like himself, and people wouldn't be poor if wealth were better distributed in the first place. He failed to spot anyone worth targeting and settled instead on buying a whole loaf of bread for a silver piece from a nearby baker.

Tearing off pieces to eat as he wandered, his attention caught on the dark obelisk standing in the middle of the square, towering over him in the golden morning light. He approached, running his fingers over the blackened stone, and found dull red writings etched into its surface — unreadable to him, and, for a moment, unsettling enough that he wondered whether it might be old blood. There was no scent of iron on it, though.

Nobody else in the square seemed to give the pillar a second glance. To them it was furniture — just another fixture in a city where people were born, grew up, worked, fell in love, married, and died. Riley found this baffling. Surely *someone* had asked questions about it before.

He approached a slender man nearby — a trader, by the look of his hands — and turned on his most winning, effeminate charm. "Hiii!"

The man looked him over, uncertain what had just happened, and made to leave.

"Please stop! I need to know! Can you tell me anything about this pillar?" Riley begged.

The man turned back, curiosity winning out. "Oh! The pillar? Oh, um — it's very old, I guess... honestly, it's just got a lot of long stories behind it. But it's just another building."

"Tell me one of the old stories, please, sir!"

"Okay. Well... um... let me think... as far as I remember, it was built during the time of [[Anparashi Puli Bralofai Blafadasi Komitani Ailushi|Puli Komitani]], around seven hundred years ago — when he refounded our glorious capital, Amphos, and moved the administration there. Apparently the writing is something he liked personally, but none of us can read it, so it's been forgotten by all but the learned."

Riley took this in — a great deal of not much, he decided — and turned on his best smile. "Thanks!" he chirped, and headed back toward the inn, cooled off and calm again.

It was half past six, and the party was due at the boat in half an hour. In the room, Ellowen and Bob remained behind while Ellowen continued her study of [[Woubble]], jotting down every gurgle and gesture in her notes.

"Woubble, tell me about yourself..." A long series of garbled noises and contortions answered her.

"Are you happy?" An elated garble.

"Would you like to go on our journey with us?" More enthusiasm still.

"Will you eat me?" A much more muted, sadder response.

"Will you eat Bob?" The same.

"Will you eat Pupolas Crate?" Bob spun around, alarmed, to watch for the answer — slower this time, more uncertain. Ellowen waved him back down.

"Can you fight?" Woubble brightened at once, gesturing his little sword-arm — though Ellowen made a mental note not to let him near danger; he was small, frail, and made of jelly, after all.

"Do you have magic?" A sad shudder answered — no.

"Would you like magic?" He looked up at her in something like awe.

"Do you have family?" A sad, uncertain gesture. *Uncertain familial relations,* she wrote.

"Do you have friends?" Confusion — the concept didn't seem to register.

"Are we your friends?" That one landed. He seemed to warm to the idea.

She began sketching him as he watched her instruments glide across the vellum, and then, hesitant but curious, reached out to touch him. His outer layer solidified and turned reflective as her hand approached, allowing her to handle him safely — she turned him over, petted him, studied his form from every angle, before resuming her questions.

"Do you like the jar?" An enthusiastic nod and happy gurgle. "See, Bob, he likes the jar!" she said pointedly, and Bob apologised to his bestie all the same.

"We have a long, and rather wet, journey ahead of us, so you may need to spend some extended periods in the jar," she explained. He seemed to understand, motioning toward it willingly.

"Can you go near water?" A long, uncertain garble — he didn't seem to know himself. She laughed softly, deciding it was probably better not to test that theory just yet — she was, after all, something of a walking drowning hazard herself when it came to magic.

She drew out [[Elements#Water Stone|her water stone]] and set it on the table near him. Woubble recoiled at once, backing away, plainly frightened. Ellowen was shocked, and a wave of pity and curiosity washed over her.

"Do you not like the stone?" she asked. Woubble retreated fully into his jar.

"It's okay, Woubble!" She quickly tucked the stone back into her cloak, and he crept back out to sit beside her while she petted him, then returned him to the jar and clipped it to her belt. She turned to Bob. "Bob, have you finally finished your gruel after so many hours?"

"Yes, bestie!" He checked with Pupolas Crate too, then, recalling how well [[Dashos]] had once trained the pup, decided to run through some commands. "Pupolas, are you able to shake your paws?" The dog rose onto his hind legs and shook obligingly.

Ellowen had an idea. "Bob, bring me your rock, please!"

Bob attempted to hurl [[Elements#Soul Stone|the black stone]] at her — it came straight back to his hand without ever touching her.

"Bob, bring your rock *over*, I mean," she sighed.

He walked it over and set it on the table at her instruction. Woubble backed toward his jar again, eyes locked on the dark stone.

"He's scared of rocks!" Bob announced, as if he'd cracked something monumental.

"No, Bob!" Ellowen laughed. "That's not it!"

"He's scared of rocks, bestie! Clearly!" He fetched his other stone — [[Frank the Rock|Frank]], currently missing his googly eyes — and set it beside the first. Woubble regarded Frank with confusion and tried, uselessly, to communicate with him. Ellowen, taken with how adorable the whole scene was, was interrupted by Bob tapping her shoulder over Frank's missing eyes.

"What?" she said, distracted — and accidentally cast Minor Illusion, conjuring the appearance of googly eyes on the rock and settling Bob at once.

Riley walked in on the scene, hissing and startling away from Pupolas Crate as any house cat would, skirting the dog carefully before joining them at the desk. "Ellowen, why are there rocks on the table? And why is the blob scared?"

"Firstly, his name is *Woubble*," she corrected. "Also — can you please place your rock on the table too, Riley? A third rock would clarify some things."

"You want me to set the table on fire?" he said, doubtful the wood would survive the attempt.

"Well, maybe that wouldn't be so smart... can you maybe walk toward him with your rock in your hand, please?" she suggested, a little stung that he'd assumed her plan hadn't been thought through.

Riley drew his fire stone out and held it palm-up, its heat constant against his skin as though his blood itself burned. He approached Woubble slowly, steadily. As he did, [[Elements#Fire Stone|his rock flared]], and his eyes rolled back. A voice echoed through his mind, placed there as though by another:

*The slime does not trust us.*

Riley wasn't sure what to make of it — he wasn't sure he trusted the rocks either, a feeling he suspected the rest of the party shared, though none of them had said so aloud. "I think... he doesn't like... the rocks," he said carefully.

"Yes, I can see that," Ellowen replied, a little dismissive but curious how he'd worked it out so fast. "But I just want to know *why*! What is it about the rocks that he dislikes so?"

"I think he may be afraid of their power," Riley offered — not certain where the thought had come from, only that it felt right.

"Is there something to be afraid of?" Ellowen's voice tightened. The stones had never been kind, exactly, but they hadn't been harmful either — not yet. Surely all power carried risk if misused; that seemed like common knowledge. Had something been missing from her education, something even the [[Head of the Arcane]] in Amphos had failed to teach her?

"I'm not sure," Riley admitted, disappointed. Curious now himself, he set the fire stone down in front of the ooze to watch him react — falling in, at last, with Ellowen's original plan. Woubble backed away from it all the same. Ellowen lunged across the table to scoop him up and free him from the experiment, cradling and soothing him as he embraced her in turn.

"It's okay. It's okay," she murmured, tucking him back into his jar, before instructing Bob and Riley to put their own stones away.

John finished loading the ship's new stores, then rushed the wagon back to the farmer — and, while the man's back was turned, dashed around the house and fixed the roof properly before his illusion could wear off. From there he returned to the inn, knocked on the party's door, and told them he'd be at the boat within the hour. They agreed. He left again for the stables, taking the Black Hare out to the fields north of the city for one last ride before several days of stillness aboard [[The Zoraneset]].

Back in the room, Ellowen finished her notes and told the others to ready themselves — reminding Bob, gently, of his new duties as captain, a role that had fallen to him following [[Mari Shkari]]'s death. Downstairs, Rhoda and Yvaquis were already waiting, thirty minutes past their intended seven o'clock departure. Yvaquis called Bob over, and he stomped down with his usual lack of ceremony. "Bestie says we're gonna get the ship now, y'coming? We're gonna — we're gonna get the ship," he said, all in one breath.

"Are we packed then, Rhoda? We need to get going. I think I'm packed!" Yvaquis said. Rhoda agreed, a little uncertainly — neither of them had much to carry, in truth. The party set off south through the city toward the harbour.

At the docks, Bob began reassigning roles with Ellowen supervising. John arrived astride the Black Hare — over five hundred kilograms of military-bred horse — uncertain whether the gangplank could bear the weight. Bob simply hopped onto the plank and beckoned him forward; it had clearly been built for exactly this kind of cargo. John led the Black Hare aboard and secured him in the hold, amidships, alongside the ship's cat and dog.

Roles were set: Captain, Big Beefy Bob, the former first mate stepping up after Mari Shkari's death; First Mate, Rhoda, whose easy charisma suited her to it; Quartermaster, Ellowen, an exceptional navigator with a scholar's grasp of cartography and international politics; Bosun, John Ironchest, a self-taught student of carpentry with strength to spare; Surgeon, Yvaquis, continuing in her old role thanks to her clerical training and her rather more informal interest in herbal remedies; Cook, Riley, inventor of the Rileywich and all its variants.

While the rest of the crew readied the ship, John set up a small stall to sell off the contents of the crate he'd recovered on the road to Pithertara — rum, herbs, and a supply of googly eyes. He'd hoped for more, but the herbs' illicit nature dampened demand, and he settled for five silver.

Bob, meanwhile, asked Ellowen which way the ship should leave. "...Away from the shore, captain..." she said, patient. He then asked if the sails were ready, and she explained — again, patiently — that this was a rowed ship; the sails only mattered once they reached open water. The first move, she told him, was tug boats to pull them clear, rowers rowing, and the anchor up. Bob agreed, assured her he'd been about to do exactly that, and attempted to haul the anchor up single-handed without using the winch, to predictably little success. Ellowen, exhausted but diplomatic, suggested he might use his crew — they were, after all, a crew. Bob called John over to help free the ship and sent Rhoda to direct the rowers. The anchor came free. Two tugboats towed them out to sea, and once clear, Rhoda's drumming brought the rowers into perfect, effortless harmony, the ship gliding forward as if by its own will. Bob had launched his "pirate" ship, bound for the coastal settlement of Myrneleu.

Below decks, Riley — utterly unaware they'd already left port — felt the kitchen implements begin sliding back and forth beneath his feet and bolted up to the top deck in a panic. "The kitchen is haunted!" he announced, to a wall of blank, unimpressed stares from everyone but Rhoda, still occupied with the rowers. He turned to Ellowen. "The kitchen is *moving*, Ellowen!"

"Riley, calm down, what's happened?" she said, hand on his shoulder.

"The kitchen is moving; stuff is moving; the ship is moving!"

"Could it not be," she said dryly, "that because the ship is moving, the wares in the kitchen are moving?"

Riley's head dropped. "Oh, yeah..." He turned back toward the stairs. "...Okay, I'm going back down."

The tugboats cast off, and the Zoraneset was alone in open water. Ellowen navigated using the map Riley had given her, employing a lodestone technique borrowed from the eastern peoples of [[As]] and [[Divine Empire of Daxing|Daxing]] — far more reliable than reading stars, birds, or fish. She called out their heading to Bob, who relayed it to Rhoda entirely wrong.

"We need to go that way," he said, pointing east, following the wind of [[Aushuzi]]. Rhoda adjusted accordingly.

Ellowen caught it at once. "No, Bob! This way!" She gestured south-west, toward the winds of [[Abstarazi]] and [[Zabirusi]]. Bob hastily passed the correction on, and Rhoda called out over her drum, "This way! Go! Go! Go! Follow the drum!"

They settled into a cruising speed of some seven miles an hour — a swift six knots, or roughly nine [[Migishli Pitukini|migishliniu]] per hour in local terms — and the long, quiet monotony of the crossing began.

Below decks, Yvaquis searched for anything of interest among a hundred and fifty bedrolls and a murky, oar-slatted gloom, driven equally by boredom and by a quiet determination not to see Bob suffer Mari Shkari's fate. She found nothing. Desperate, she called out to the rowers. "Uh, sorry guys! I know you're busy rowing, and probably sweating, but have any of you got any herbs?"

"We're in tunics! Do you think we have any pockets?" one rower snapped back.

She found John tending to the Black Hare among them and put the question to him too. "Herbs? For medicine? No. You should know," he said.

"No — herbs for smoking, y'know, weed?"

"No! You think I would corrupt myself with such substances? Sorry, I have no idea," he said, disgusted.

She gave up and returned topside, asking everyone she passed the same question, without luck, until she reached the kitchen — where Riley handed her basil and oregano, not remotely what she was after. He went back to making garlic bread.

John spent the afternoon training the Black Hare — recall, mostly — and mixing up a concoction of tanning oil and health potions between apple treats. Bob and Ellowen worked the navigation together, Bob pausing to teach Pupolas Crate a trick or two, including how to hold a dagger in his mouth.

"Bestie! Bestie! Can I have one of your daggers — the one you never use — to give to Pupolas for stab stab, please!?" he asked, as though the request were both reasonable and well considered.

"Why do you need the dagge—" Ellowen started.

"I'm teaching Pupolas — teaching Pupolas how to be a Big Beefy Manly Dog," Bob barrelled on.

"What are you going to do with the dagger and the dog?"

"I am gonna teach Pupolas. Pupolas is going to be a big beefy manly dog." He offered nothing further, which somehow made Ellowen more nervous, not less.

"Okay... please look after this with your life, and with Pupolas's life too!" She handed over an ornamental Brackenfallian dagger with deep reluctance. Bob, by sheer luck, placed the hilt — not the blade — in Pupolas's mouth, then took up his own axe the same way and charged the ship's mast to demonstrate, the puppy mimicking him with great enthusiasm.

Ellowen, a little rattled by the display, checked on Woubble instead, and found him faintly seasick in his jar. Feeling for the poor ooze, she cast Sleep over him, and he drifted off.

The sun hung clear and the sea stayed calm. As it began to set, a shimmer caught Rhoda's eye from beneath the waves — white, not the orange-red of the sinking sun. She talked the party through slowing the ship without stopping outright, lest inertia strand them off course, and then went overboard herself, shifting into a form closer to merfolk. What she saw was unmistakably home: the [[Shimmering Moonrise Reefs]], and beyond them, [[The Shimmering Moontide]] itself. Too far to reach without losing hours, maybe the whole ship's bearings — she lingered too long regardless, and had to scramble back for the trailing rope, nearly missing it, dragged along the surface as she called for help. Ellowen shouted across the deck for anyone, then specifically for the captain, and Bob hauled her back aboard with the rest of the crew's help. On dry planking, Rhoda shifted back to her human shape.

Dinner followed — Riley's work, sixty percent of a barrel of grain going into bread, puls, and gruel for the whole crew, some of it lost to his usual scrappiness with burns and misplaced dishes. He invented toasted garlic sandwiches along the way, stretching what remained. Bob, hearing the word gruel, went below for a bowl each for himself, Ellowen, and Pupolas — a detour that distracted Ellowen from the star chart long enough to send the ship off course, south and west of the Shimmering, and add a full day to the journey.

That evening, John practiced his poses on the top deck, where Yvaquis found him.

"Hi, John! What are you doing?" she asked, knowing perfectly well.

"Do you need something?" he said, mildly put out by the interruption.

"Oh, no, nothing... but I did hear the other day that you had some kind of manuscript. Is there a chance I could have a nosey?"

"Sure." He dug through his bag without hesitation and handed it over.

"Thank you! I'll have a read through and give it back at some point."

Ellowen found Bob at the bow, dangling Pupolas Crate out over the siphon toward the sea.

"Bob, maybe we should rest, y'know? The pup looks like he could rest," she suggested, watching the puppy squirm, exhausted from a day of throwing his head against wood with a dagger between his teeth.

"He's doing great \[undiscernible noises\]. He's a big dog and is growing big. He's fine," Bob said, missing every hint.

"Yes, he is — and you know what a big pup will require? Rest." She set the puppy down; Pupolas dropped instantly into sleep. "Captain! Captain Bob! Bestie! How do you feel like today has gone?"

"Boat," Bob said, with total conviction. "It's been very ship. We've really been sailing on these seas today. Very pirate-like. Not many pirate interactions but I'm feeling very pirate-like. Yeah — pirates." It came out as an enthusiastic, grammar-optional torrent of nouns, but the joy behind it was unmistakable.

"I just have a feeling that something's not quite right," Ellowen admitted. "I have a feeling that for some reason we're just not getting much closer to our destination, and I'm not quite sure why." The stars seemed off. The water felt strange. Even her lodestone seemed to be pointing wrong.

Bob looked around, uncomprehending. "We are definitely, still, I think, heading around water." To him, boat on water surrounded by water looked the same everywhere, so surely it must all lead the same place.

Ellowen gave up on the navigation lesson. "Also, Bob — has your rock been speaking to you at all?"

"No. My rock? It's a *rock*!" Bob said, aghast at the very idea — rocks didn't have mouths, after all, they didn't move, they certainly didn't talk.

"I see," Ellowen said, certain he simply hadn't understood the question. "Well — if you hear anything that's not normal, you'd let me know, wouldn't you, Bob?"

"Of course, bestie!"

"How do you feel about the people who've joined us?" she asked next.

"Lovely! The guy is really, really super strong, and the mermaid lady is probably really strong, and as long as they're big and beefy, I don't mind. Yeah, they seem lovely!"

"I think Woubble..." She lifted the sleeping ooze from his beaker, holding him toward Bob without quite handing him over. "I think he doesn't like the rocks too much."

"Has he tried googly eyes as well?" Bob asked, apparently having forgotten last night's experiment entirely. "They could be his little friends, bestie! They could look at him and he could look at them and they could really form a connection!"

"I think he gets confused over googly eyes," Ellowen said. "Plus — you're talking about forming a connection with a rock?"

"You got an issue with rocks?" Bob said, suddenly defensive of Frank and his other stone, both still missing an eye apiece.

"Do you have an issue with your bestie, Bob?" Ellowen pushed back. He went quiet. She softened. "Well — maybe you and Pupolas should get some rest. I can take over here for a bit while you all sleep."

"You can do some ship stuff! Be very pirate-like! I'm gonna head off!" He stomped away, confident as ever, and settled Pupolas into their quarters.

Riley climbed to the top deck and stood on the starboard side, staring out over the water, lost in thought. His stone flared hot in his hand and spoke, unbidden:

*In the forest, you will learn.*

He looked down, startled to find the stone already in his palm, and waited. Nothing more came. He stood there a while longer before retreating to his bedroll, turning the stone over in his hands, enamoured and confused in equal measure.

Ellowen returned to her work near the bow, sketching Woubble as she had the night before, before pulling out her own stone.

"Speak to me!" she demanded.

Nothing.

She was disappointed — the stone plainly held something important, knowledge, power, some pure and uncorrupted fragment of the past, and she deserved to know what. She simply *wanted* to know. She began to meditate, pouring her magic into it instead.

It glowed. It spoke.

*"Have you learned?"*
*"What is it you would like me to learn?"*
*"The wind will call."*
*"Which direction will the wind call?"*
*"South. Abstarazi."*
*"Why does my friend not like you?"*
*"..."*
*"Should I trust you?"*
*"..."*
*"What does the deep water mean?"*
*"Fate will tell."*

Her magic ran dry, and the trance broke on its own, leaving her exhausted. The stone dimmed. Woubble, startled awake, looked terrified; she shifted the stone to her opposite hip.

Rhoda, who'd watched the whole thing, came over. "Why was the rock glowing, and what was with that weird look on your face?" Before this, the stones had seemed harmless enough — now she'd just watched Ellowen burn through real effort trying to talk to hers.

"It's just part of my meditation," Ellowen said, keeping the truth close. It struck her, not for the first time, that she and her stone might share more than she'd like in that respect.

Yvaquis drifted over, having overheard, and showed Rhoda her own stone again. Rhoda asked — not for the first time — how one came to have one at all; she felt, increasingly, like she needed one herself. No one had a satisfying answer. It seemed the stones chose their bearers, not the other way around — the temple, for the first four; the open road, for John.

Yvaquis lifted her stone and tried to mirror what Ellowen had done, pouring herself into it. Her eyes rolled back, her lids fluttered, tears slid down her cheeks as she fell backward without ever releasing the glowing green stone. Darkness swallowed her perception entirely — nothing to see, nothing to hear, an absolute, total loneliness. Then, a voice, placed directly in her mind rather than heard from anywhere at all:

*You are not in the right place.*

She woke alone and terrified, tear-streaked, and immediately tried to press for clarification — mentally? physically? — and got nothing back. She tried again, forcing herself back under. The rock responded, harsher this time, as if displeased at being coerced.

*"You are not in the right place."*
*"Is there anything else you want to tell me?"*
*"You are too distant for me to hear."*

She surfaced again, disappointed and hollow, the same loneliness clinging to her thoughts. Rhoda, seeing her face, struck up a gentle tune to try to lift her spirits.

Watching Yvaquis go under a second time, Ellowen tried once more herself — meditating, pushing energy toward the stone, failing to enter the trance and only tiring herself further. Frustration mounted. Then it flickered, and dizziness took her, and with a single held thought she slipped into the link after all.

*"Are we going the right way?"*
*"Your journey is correct."*
*"Are we on course?"*
*"You will arrive where you need to arrive."*
*"We will reach our destination at the intended time?"*
*"You will be there when you need to be there."*
*"Where is it I can find another one of you?"*
*"It will come in time."*
*"Will it be when we reach the coast?"*
*"When the winds blow north. Toward [[Gorasi]]."*

She surfaced and checked her lodestone at once — the wind was blowing west. She checked again, then the waters, the birds, the stars, and confirmed it: the ship was well off course. She leapt up and corrected their heading herself, cutting a full day's delay down to four hours. Unsatisfied, and ignoring the unease still sitting in her chest, she went back under to confront the stone directly.

*"Why is it that you did not let me know that we were on the wrong course?"*
*"You were always on the right course."*

That answer irritated her more than any silence could have. She pressed on.

*"Why is it that we repel each other? Why is it we cannot be in the same circle?"*
*"Once were one. Once were eight."*
*"Eight? I thought there are seven stones?"*
*"Now seven."*
*"Can you tell me about the man who went up the mountain?"*
*"Many have trodden the path."*
*"Have many been successful?"*
*"There have been two... now will be three."*
*"What is success?"*
*"Once were one. Once were eight. Now are seven."*
*"Where is it that we find the other two?"*
*"You have already found one. You have already found two."*
*"Where is it that we would find the other stones that we do not already possess?"*
*"You have already found one. You will find the other when winds blow north."*
*"Do you know this friend of mine?"*
*"The slime does not trust us."*
*"Why does he not trust you?"*
*"..."*
*"Should I trust you?"*
*"...Hasharshagetes trusted. The Puli trusted. Atiri trusted."*
*"But should they have trusted you?"*
*"...They all lived long."*

Ellowen forced herself out of the trance, her trust in the stone badly shaken. Not once had it given her a straight answer — only riddles, and the growing possibility of deceit. Rather than pour more of herself into it, she turned instead to studying the magic itself, testing it for corruption. She found none. It was pure, elemental, untouched — as close to natural as magic could be. The best she could describe it was flowing water, moving freely through the world's own current.

The party settled in to rest. John made sure the Black Hare was comfortable in the hold. Riley lay in his bedroll, still turning his stone over in his hands. Bob slept curled up with Pupolas. Yvaquis and Rhoda talked quietly on the top deck until sleep took them both. Ellowen stayed there too, meditating.

The Soul Stone began to glow, and in Bob's mind, screaming rose up — before fading beneath a single, familiar voice. The [[First Puli|First Puli]].

*"Bob, do you remember me? I have spoken to you before. I am the First Puli."*
*"Puli bestie?! Buddy! Buddy! Hey, buddy! What up?"*
*"You must keep heading south."*
*"I must keep heading south."*
*"The winds will blow north there."*
*"Bob doesn't know directions."*
*"You will come together."*

Unconscious in her own meditation, Ellowen's hand rose of its own accord and cast Light, a small beacon blooming over the pitch-black water above the sleeping forms of Yvaquis and Rhoda. And in her trance, her book began to speak on its own, reciting something new.

>Itrea was beautiful.
>A cruel lesson: beauty and suffering often share a roof.
>The temples gleamed like the teeth of a beast that meant to swallow me.
>My masters beat me as one swats a fly
>—even when the priests and laws forbade it.
>But law is a thin shield when one holds no name.
