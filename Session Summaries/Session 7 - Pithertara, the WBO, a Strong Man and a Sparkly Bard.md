_[[Setting/Semantics/Dating/index|4th Hanomi, 1782 IOG]]_

[[The Zoraneset]] came into [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Pithertara|Pithertara]] a little after half past one, her hull settling against the southern docks where the gulls were loudest and the smell of tar and brine was thickest. It was the same stretch of stone quay where, not so long ago, [[Dashos|Dashos]] had been dragged off in irons, and where [[Masha|Masha]] had been put to death by the city watch for the fire she'd set at [[The Great Palace of Amphos|The Great Palace in Amphos]]. Nobody said as much aloud. Nobody needed to.

[[Evangeline Marquis|Yvaquis]] was gone before the gangplank had properly settled, off to collect her herbs — her _illicit substances_, if anyone asked — leaving [[Ellowen Aurelian Vale|Ellowen]], [[Riley - Player Notes|Riley]], and [[Big Beefy Bob - Player Notes|Big Beefy Bob]] to take their first proper look at the city.

Pithertara was enormous. Eight thousand souls or more, dwarfing every settlement they'd passed since [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Amphos|Amphos]] itself, though even Pithertara bowed to the capital in size. It was a coastal city in the old style: the harbour and the drinking houses hugging the shore, the noble keep and the city watch's barracks a little further west where the land curled southward, and beyond that, deeper into the city, the great open square where markets and spectacles alike were held.

It didn't take long to work out that something was happening there today. Port workers and passing travellers spoke of nothing else — a strongman exhibition. Yvaquis, catching up with the others, jerked her head toward the noise and said simply, "Let's go over there."

Ellowen didn't need telling twice. She looped her arm through Big Beefy Bob's and skipped the both of them off toward the square, half-dragging him along in her enthusiasm.

There was a reason they'd stopped in Pithertara at all, of course, beyond curiosity — the ship needed restocking. [[Pupolas Crate]] and [[Thumpwick]] had gotten into the stores somewhere between here and their last port in [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Aklebe|Aklebe]] and helped themselves rather liberally, much to the fury of their now-late captain, [[Mari Shkari]]. But that errand could wait. The drums were getting louder.

As they neared the square, the crowd thickened and grew rougher — more elbows, more shouting, less patience. Drums thundered under a rising wall of singing and screaming voices.

Riley, nursing a stomach that had been complaining at him all morning, decided this was the moment to slip off on his own. He melted into the press of bodies with surprising success, drawing no attention at all — right up until the smell of meat found him and led him, nose first, to a butcher's stall piled with cuts for the festival crowd.

He reached for it. He tripped. The entire stall went over, meat scattering across the cobbles in every direction. Riley managed to salvage exactly one item from the wreckage — a single rasher of raw, uncooked bacon — and turned to bolt.

He didn't get far. The stallholder didn't shout for the watch, or for event security, or for anyone sensible. He shouted for the strongmen.

Only one answered.

Riley looked up and understood, immediately and completely, why the man had come alone. Seven feet of him, built with the kind of precision that seemed less born than _engineered_ — every muscle exactly where it ought to be, less than five percent of him anything but muscle, dressed in nothing but metal bracers and a scale-mail thong trimmed in an odd, unplaceable purple. His skin was the colour of a well-polished bronze doorknob, entirely hairless but for an immaculate handlebar moustache.

"Mr. Strongman! Mr. Strongman!" the butcher cried.

"What do you need?" the strongman said.

"There's a young woman here, trying to steal my food! She absolutely destroyed my stall!"

Riley scrambled to his feet to run. He didn't make it two steps before a massive hand closed on the scruff of his neck and hauled him back off the ground entirely. He looked the man up and down, desperate, and offered the only bargaining chip he had.

"Do you want an apple?"

"You can't win me with food. I only eat for protein," the strongman said.

Riley held out the rasher of bacon instead.

"Do you really think a single rasher of raw bacon will satisfy me?" The strongman looked, if anything, insulted by the suggestion.

It was at this point that Ellowen — hearing the commotion, spotting Riley dangling by the scruff — came dashing over with Bob in tow, Yvaquis a step behind.

"What is wrong? What is the matter here?" she demanded.

Riley managed one word, pointing frantically. "Strongman."

Ellowen's eyes found the man holding her friend, and climbed, and kept climbing, until they finally reached his face. She held her ground anyway. "Put our friend down."

"Have you not seen the wreck this young woman has caused?" the strongman said, justice ringing plainly in his voice.

"Well, we shall clear it up," Ellowen said, with total sincerity and not the faintest idea of how.

"Clear it up? The meat is on the floor! It's covered in dirt!"

Big Beefy Bob, wanting only to help and thinking of his favourite meal in the world, offered brightly, "We can make gruel! Everyone loves gruel! We can have chocolate gruel!"

"This man is a butcher," the strongman pointed out, with some patience. "Chocolate gruel will not satisfy this man, nor can he sell such a commodity."

Undeterred, Bob tried again. "Meaty flavoured gruel!"

Ellowen, tired of the whole conversation, walked over to the ruined stall, muttered something under her breath, and set to work — Mage Hand lifting the fallen meat back into place, Prestidigitation scouring the stall until it gleamed, cleaner than it had been before Riley ever touched it. She turned back around, thoroughly pleased with herself.

"There you go, good sir. Now please release my friend here."

Still holding Riley, the strongman turned to the butcher, wanting to see justice through to its proper end. "Is this solution sufficient, or do you want something more from me?" Ellowen shot the butcher a look that left very little room for a second opinion. The butcher, throat working, suggested instead that the strongman ought to get back to his post — "you can't be away from the show for too long, can you?"

Rather than lowering Riley gently, the strongman simply let go. Riley dropped a full foot to the cobbles, hood knocked askew in the fall — and with it, for the whole watching crowd to see, his cat's ears popped free.

As the strongman turned to walk away, Ellowen called after him. "Good sir! Please wait! I'm sorry for this misunderstanding, but I would like to introduce you to my... team..."

"To your team? Why would I need to know of your team? I have no time, I'm sorry, citizen."

Rebuffed, Ellowen turned to Bob, more offended on principle than anything else. "Bob... that guy!"

Bob considered this with unusual gravity. "Yeah. He is Big and Beefy, but he has not got a Big and Beefy attitude."

"No — he is not quite big and beefy with a hat!" Ellowen agreed.

"We've got to find him a hat," Bob decided.

Riley, wiping at the bronzer now running down his cheek and matting the fur of his ears, offered his own assessment. "He is very greasy."

The exhibition itself was about to begin. The music swelled — faster now, grander, dressed up in fanfare and drama. Riley pulled out his lute and wove himself into the procession, playing in the traditional Amphosian style, drawing a small audience of his own.

One set of percussion, though, refused to blend in. It came from just beside the stage — increasingly erratic, increasingly cacophonic, entirely out of step with everything around it. Not wrong, exactly. Just _other_. A different rhythm. Perhaps a different musical language altogether.

Ellowen tracked the noise to its source: a woman standing beside the stage where no performer had any business standing, fiery red hair, dressed in colours that belonged nowhere near this coast, drumming with total, unshakeable confidence, as though she were the only musician in the square. If there had been no other music playing, it might have passed for minstrelsy. As it was, it simply added noise to noise.

Ellowen, intrigued despite herself, approached. "Oh! What a beautiful piece you are playing! May I ask from where you have come, and from where you have learned this music?"

The woman answered with a slur, but a sincere one. "It just comes naturally. It flows out of my body."

Before Ellowen could press further, the crowd erupted — not for the drummer, but for the stage, where every gaze in the square turned as one, as if compelled. The music cut. Fire-breathers lit the air in front of the stage as four strongmen were called up one by one: Dunfrith Kingston, Woolbert Lightning, Evergar Kinsey, and — the name that snapped the party's attention like a struck bell — [[John Ironchest - Player Notes|John Ironchest]].

The first event was the atlas stones race. John Ironchest won by a wide margin.

The second stage of the competition opened the floor to challengers: anyone who could survive five rounds wrestling against Ironchest himself would earn a place in the remaining events. Ellowen turned to Bob at once.

"Bob, you could do this! Plus, it gives you a chance to show up this John guy."

Bob agreed without a shred of hesitation. Ellowen looked him over, decided he wasn't sufficiently intimidating, and cast a spell to leave him bronzed, oiled, and gleaming before sending him up onto the stage.

Round one: Ironchest put Bob flat on the boards. Bob got back up. Round two: Ironchest himself dropped to a knee but flung Bob backward regardless. By round three, Bob was visibly losing ground, so Ellowen — watching from the crowd — cast Grease beneath his feet to buy him an edge. It worked, after a fashion: greased and sliding, Bob lost round four outright when Ironchest slipped straight past him. In the fifth and final round, Bob went down, slid, and somehow hauled himself upright again in the last instant before the bell.

Five rounds survived. Ironchest let out a roar to the crowd in a show of raw strength — and immediately turned to the referees with an accusation.

"Someone had greased my opponent and the stage. I also could sense traces of magic in this grease. Please investigate and be more vigilant. I do not want my reputation tarnished by some cheaters."

No one was caught.

The Atlas Squats came next: each competitor had to raise a four-hundred-and-ten-kilogram stone and squat with it held in their hands, most repetitions winning. Dunfrith Kingston couldn't lift it at all and forfeited. Neither could Bob. Woolbert Lightning managed two squats; Evergar Kinsey managed seven. Ironchest managed twelve, and took the round easily.

The keg toss followed, and here Bob had his moment — still smarting from the squats, he channelled his frustration into fury and hurled his keg clean over the heads of the crowd, further than any other competitor, to general astonishment.

Amid the cheering, Riley found a stray pocket to dip into and came away with a single silver piece.

The final event was the deadlift. Both Bob and Ironchest failed their first attempt — and when the announcers examined Ironchest's stone, they discovered it had been chained to the ground. Sabotage. As the crowd murmured, five strongmen hauled a full one-tonne stone onto the stage, the wooden boards groaning and bending beneath its weight as it was set down at Ironchest's feet. He examined it, took hold, and lifted it with what looked like very little effort at all.

Strongest man in all the lands. Again.

Ironchest ran through his victory poses — and with each one, the [[Elements#Power Stone|purple trim of his scale-mail]] lit up in perfect rhythm, glowing brighter and brighter. And, impossibly, [[Elements#Elemental Stones|every stone]] the party carried answered it. Ellowen saw [[Elements#Water Stone|hers]] flare to life beneath her cloak. Riley felt the sudden warmth of [[Elements#Fire Stone|his own]]. And in Big Beefy Bob's hand, the voice that lived inside [[Elements#Soul Stone|his stone]] spoke a single word directly into his mind.

_Power._

The red-haired minstrel drifted over to Yvaquis, watching all this with open fascination. "How odd that he is glowing purple, and your rucksack is glowing green! I wish I could have something like that." Yvaquis tried to explain — [[Elements#Earth Stone|her own magical rock]], though she couldn't say why it glowed, or how a man on a stage came to have one too. Later, once the show had ended, Bob took it upon himself to walk the minstrel through the whole story with the aid of [[Bob's Travelling Picture Book Journal|his crudely-drawn picture book]] — and in doing so, made it fairly clear to everyone listening just how little the party actually understood about the stones. What they were. Where they'd come from. How they worked, or why there were so few of them in the world. Just that they were, and that was that.

Bob went to find Ironchest himself, curious about the stone's behaviour. Ironchest, still riding high off his win, spent a while simply complimenting Bob on his performance and lost the thread of the question entirely, so Bob asked again.

"I like your stone. It's a nice purply colour. Where did you get it?"

"The stone? To be honest, I don't really know what it is either," Ironchest admitted. "It almost clung to me when I saw it, on my journey here. I know some things about this stone... I discovered a strange obelisk-like structure in the forest. It was embedded in the ground. It seemed to have the same effects as my stone, but I wasn't sure what it was."

"What are the effects of your stone?"

Rather than explain, Ironchest simply showed him — a firm dap of the hands, and a jolt of raw power surged through Bob, leaving him, briefly, bigger and beefier than before.

That drew the rest of the party over fast. Yvaquis went first. "How far away is this forest from where we are now?"

"Pretty far. It was a treacherous journey. I came on a boat."

Ellowen, thinking of another forest and another obelisk from before Aklebe, asked, "Was there a magical tree in that forest?"

"There was a magical tree."

Riley pushed harder. "John, tell me about the tree!"

"Why should I tell you about the tree?" Ironchest replied, a little insulted at the demand.

"We saw a tree," Riley pressed, drink loosening his tongue. "A magical tree. Magical rocks. Magical tree. Tell me about your magical tree."

Ironchest went still. "So you have rocks as well?"

"Yeah," Yvaquis confirmed.

Curiosity got the better of him. "Show me yours."

Riley obliged, opening his palm to reveal a stone the deep red of a ruby or a blood-emerald, hot to any touch but his own — a wonder it hadn't scorched through his clothes by now. Ironchest examined it and reached out, fingers extended toward it, and found that no matter how far he pushed, he could not quite close the distance to touch it.

The minstrel, stumbling closer, fell toward him, hand outstretched for anything to grab — and landed squarely at his groin, near enough his stone that her hand was flung backward as if struck.

Ellowen had taken her own stone out to study it by then, and the minstrel tried her luck there too, reaching, straining, never quite able to bridge the last inch between her fingers and the stone.

Bob pulled his stone out as well — still missing the googly eyes he'd originally stuck to it, quietly removed by Ellowen's Mage Hand while he slept, and just as quietly disguised again now with a hasty Minor Illusion so he wouldn't notice. The minstrel asked if she might touch it. Bob, entirely serious, told her to ask the rock itself. She did. Bob decided, after a moment's consideration, that the rock had said yes. She reached out anyway — and, like all the others, could not touch it.

Disheartened, she fell back on what she knew best and sang to the stones instead — a melancholy, pleading song, hoping to charm her way into their favour. The stones glowed warmly in response to the music. They still would not let her near.

Ironchest decided it was time to report back to the [[World Bodybuilding Order|WBO]]. As he made to leave, a scrawny young man — mid-teens by the look of him, though he carried himself far older — came sprinting up, calling, "Sir! Sir! Sir!"

Ironchest sighed. "Old Ricky..."

"Yes sir! It's me, [[Ulriki]]!" the boy said, entirely unbothered by the tone.

Riley, baffled, asked the air in general, "Who is Ulriki? Why did John call him Old Ricky?" No one answered.

"Old Ricky, what are you doing here? I left you at the inn," Ironchest said.

Ulriki, breathless and flustered, held out a bundle of crumpled, burnt parchment tied with scorched twine — ancient-looking, though clearly modern cargo. "Yes... yes but... I've gone through that crate... the one you found on the boat we drove into the shore... there's this... there's [[Manuscript on Tabaxi|this manuscript]]... It's about history, or something... it's really burnt... got tonnes of words missing... it seems to describe some cat-like folk..."

Riley's ears pricked up.

"It's about... their histories within... [[Empire of Solara|the empire]]..." Ulriki went on.

Ellowen, using the private hand-sign code she and Riley had worked out between themselves, flagged this as worth their attention.

Ulriki pushed through the rest of it. "It's quite comprehensive, but it seems to describe that there are only a small number in the empire. In the capital, even — in the last census there was just two!"

"Two!?" Ironchest exclaimed.

"Yes, but it didn't include the slums," Ulriki said.

Ironchest looked at Riley, plainly aware by now of what he was. Ellowen did the maths before anyone else — if the slums weren't counted, that meant at least three Tabaxi in the capital, and Riley had never so much as met another of his own kind before [[Tabtab - Player Notes|Tabtab]]. The same thought landed in Yvaquis's mind and Ironchest's a moment later.

Ulriki wasn't finished. "The manuscript also said that a guard from the palace in Amphos, he took a baby cat-folk from the palace, placed it in a barn in the slums, and left him there to die. No food or water or anything. This happened approximately eighteen years prior — but this manuscript is three years old. This child could be eighteen, or nineteen, or even twenty now!"

The party went very quiet. Ulriki added, almost as an afterthought, that the manuscript itself was clearly important and rightfully belonged to the Bureau of Imperial Records in Amphos — he had no idea what it was doing here. Riley's hands went to his daggers, fury with nowhere to land.

Ironchest thanked Ulriki, who suggested he come along to the tavern — there were people there, city guards who'd overheard him reading it earlier, who might know more than he did. Ellowen, wary, asked exactly who these people were. Guards, John told her. Soldiers of the Empire of Solara.

She pressed him further — what did he intend to do now? Would he join them, on their ship, on whatever journey lay ahead? He turned the question over. Perhaps the stones led somewhere. Perhaps they led to what he wanted. Ellowen asked him plainly what that was.

"Strength," he said. Someone who could truly rival him. Someone who might bring out something greater.

He went back to the WBO's tent, letter of invitation in hand, and confronted the official there. "You said there would be competition here. That I would find challenge. That I would find someone to rival me here — and yet there was nothing! No one stood up to me!"

"Sorry, but you are simply unrivalled between us and our members," the official said.

Ironchest, disgusted that a mere bystander had nearly outdone some of the Order's own men, asked for his prize. Twenty gold was pressed into his hand.

Riley, meanwhile, had slipped into the tent after him — badly — and was caught almost immediately by the official behind the counter, who alerted John. Riley tapped him on the shoulder anyway, laying on the charm. "Oh, John, you were so strong earlier! I saw that burnt manuscript that you have and wondered if I could borrow it." Ironchest didn't bite. He told Riley to leave; he was trespassing.

Outside, Ellowen turned to Bob. "Hey, Bob! I think Riley is in a bit of trouble with that man and needs some help. He would really like that book, and I think it's best that we help him."

"Yeah, we should help him get that book from our really big and beefy manly friend," Bob agreed.

"I believe in you! You can do this! You've got a great shot! You're the biggest, strongest person I know!" Ellowen said, and — perhaps a little too enthusiastically — cast a Minor Illusion that left Bob standing seven-foot-two, a full two inches taller than Ironchest himself.

Bob strode into the tent, towering over Riley, and went straight on the offensive. "Hey, big beefy manly manly friend man! That book you have there, manly manly friend man — what's that book you have there, big beefy manly manly friend man!"

"As you heard from the kid, it's a burnt manuscript from a boat I helped save someone from. It has details about a cat-race," Ironchest said.

Bob glanced between Riley and Ellowen. "Why do we need this?" Riley, exasperated, pointed at his own ears.

Understanding dawned. Bob tried again. "Hey, big beefy manly manly friend man, may I please look at your big beefy manly book? Please?"

"This was given to me. It was a reward for saving someone's life. I don't desire to part with it," Ironchest said.

"I don't want to throw it," Bob pushed on. "I just want to read it so then I can give it to my besties, who can read it, then give it back to you. I can even give you my hat" — he gestured to [[Big Beefy Bob's Little Orange Hat|his beloved tiny orange hat]] — "then you can give me the book, and once you get the book back, I can get my hat back."

Ironchest relented. "Well... it's nothing important to me..." He tossed the manuscript to Bob. "I don't want your hat, keep it." He picked Riley up by the scruff again and carried him out.

Bob, being unable to read, made a valiant attempt anyway — sounding out letters, getting most of them wrong, dragging his way barely halfway through before Ellowen called to him from outside the tent. "Bob, come here! I can help you!"

"I'm reading, bestie! I'm doing it!" Bob called back, delighted with himself.

"I know, but I can help you!"

"I'm good at reading!"

"Yes! I know you are, bestie! I would like to read it myself. Can you show me how well you read?"

Bob, gamely: "_b- l- o- o- d- e- d- blue-ded- t- a- b- a- x- i- tab-axe-eye._"

"Ah, yes, bestie! I'm sure that that's exactly what it says," Ellowen said, holding out her hand. "Could I maybe have a closer look at that, bestie, as we are such best friends."

"We are, aren't we!" Bob agreed happily, then, in a sudden burst, "Wait, wait, wait, wait, wait, wait. Manly, manly, manly, manly, manly best friend!" — this last shouted after Ironchest, now walking off toward the tavern with Ulriki. "May I please share this with my bestest bestie best friend?"

"Sure," Ironchest called back, not turning around.

Bob handed the manuscript over.

Ellowen had already memorised the whole thing by the time it reached her hands — but reading it had left her certain that Riley, not she, needed to be the one to actually hold it. The trick was getting Bob to agree without making him go ask John's permission all over again.

"Hey, bestie! Isn't Riley a tabaxi?" she tried.

Bob blinked. "What's a tabaxi?"

"A cat! A tabaxi cat!"

"He does, uh... he has cat ears?"

"Yes! Like the lady we found — in the ears with [[The Prince - Player Notes|the pri— your poor bestie]]! Your poor best friend... with the gruel!"

Bob lit up at the mention. "Ah, yes! My poor bestie! Cat lady with the poor bestie! Was some good gruel. I wonder how he's doing!"

"I'm sure he's fine," Ellowen said, steering back. "But Riley should read this — I'm sure he'd like to know more about his lineage." Bob remained politely baffled; the cult hadn't exactly encouraged introspection. Ellowen tried a different angle. "As we are all such best friends, and good friends, and reading this — surely we should let Riley read it too, to take part in the group fun and help him! And you trust me, don't you, bestie?"

That did it. Whatever else Bob was uncertain about, friendship was not negotiable, and he gave in completely. Ellowen turned to Riley.

"Riley — would you like to learn your history?"

Riley took the manuscript from her hands and began working through the phonetic script himself. The party, none of them having eaten all day, felt the hunger set in properly for the first time.

Ironchest and Ulriki made their way back toward the tavern — which now, thanks to a freshly painted sign depicting John hoisting an atlas stone overhead, bore a new name: The Iron Chest. Ulriki, walking alongside, filled his patron in at length on everyone who'd been crowded around trying to make sense of the manuscript.

The rest of the party, famished and low on supplies, set off after them — but not before a stop at the market for bread, hardtack, grain, and meat. Riley, flush with money from Aklebe, spent some of it on food for the homeless of Pithertara, buying bread and raw meat in the hope it would find its way to a few hungry mouths in the slums. Bob, meanwhile, shouldered the bulk of the ship's cargo himself: five barrels of bread, one barrel of grain, two barrels of dried meat, and more besides — eight barrels in total, hauled at once, straight to the Zoraneset, hurled into the hold to the shock of the crew, before he scooped up Pupolas Crate (so the animal wouldn't get at the new stores) and sprinted back to the square. Between what he'd carried and what remained, the crew of two hundred and fifty now had enough to make the two-day run on the dromon toward their next stop — closer to Itrea than they had ever been.

Inside the Iron Chest, John and Ulriki found their way to the table Ulriki had shared earlier with a trio of Solari watchmen. As they sat, the conversation already underway drifted over.

"Being in the military of the Empire of Solara is so difficult," one watchman was saying. "I'm at Amphos one day, the next I'm at Pithertara, then I'm posted to [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Mormegalia|Mormegalia]] — and now all the sudden we're being posted to [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Itrea|Itrea]]!? What is this guy thinking?"

The watchmen spotted Ulriki rejoining them. "Ah! Ulriki! You're back! Have you got that book? It was such a fun read!"

"I love reading about [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/NPCs/Amphos/The Court of Amphos/Zenshoran Hagashar|our boss, the head of the military]]!" another added. "Sometimes he's difficult to understand. He has a really thick accent, y'know!"

Ironchest, mixing up [[Empire of Solara|Solara]] and [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Solaum|Solaum]] in his head, asked, "I am interested about the Obelisk in Solaum. Do you know anything about that?"

"Well, no one in our military has been there in a very long time — probably about seven hundred years — but let us know if you find someone!"

At this point the rest of the party arrived. Riley took a seat at the table just behind, back turned, hood up, and settled in to listen without drawing attention. Ironchest, absorbed in conversation, never noticed him.

The watchman continued, laying out the geography in one long, practiced breath: "Solaum is on the other side of the sea, then walk a long expanse through several independent powers. There's always the more direct river route through [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Rhithon|Rhithon]], south-west of here, but you still have to cross the [[Moli Aeianao]] first. From Rhithon you boat down the [[River Kadarai]]; at the end you should join the [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Leicearis|Leicearis]] Road, head west to [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Docealy|Docealy]], then join the [[River Koupakropoi]], boating to the mouth — you should end up in the [[Moli Aurirorinao]]. Take a ship through here, or stick to the south shore until you reach [[Polbaridaci]]. Take the west exit from the city and follow the road; you'll see the ring road from there. Head north, avoiding the ring, and you'll end up at Solaum. If you want to see a similar obelisk, there's one in Itrea. It's the holiest city in all the world! You should be able to find whatever you seek there. It's just across the Moli Aeianao — most boats go there from here, it's a very common pilgrimage destination."

"And Solaum is where?" Ironchest pressed.

"Across the sea too — west instead of south. I'd personally say head to Itrea instead. It's a much easier journey, just be sure to stick to the coast — the sea's very treacherous, full of merfolk."

"Is the obelisk in Itrea different to the one in Solaum? I heard the one in Solaum grants power."

A third watchman chimed in. "There are old, very old, very confused stories from long, long ago. I'm sure it's changed a lot over the millennia, y'know how people are, adding flourishes and such — but it's said that the water, or the flame, changes when near. People feel their blood move oddly, their spit change, their sweat boil off their skin. I've been there before myself, felt odd, but never seen anything like that."

"You both forgot to mention," the third said, "[[Obelisk of Origin|the Obelisk of Origin]] in Solaum is almost impossible to reach. You have to make it to the centre, where people are always crowded — it's a two-mile walk from the outermost outskirts to the centre, assuming you don't get lost, mugged, or killed along the way. Knowing me, I'd get distracted by the glory of the city before I even made it."

"Yes, well, it is a difficult journey — one that may take months," the first said. "Itrea's more desirable anyway. It's also home to the entrance of the [[Mount Aemi]] mountain trial. People attempt the walk all the time, but few make it to the top without turning back for one reason or another."

Ironchest, intrigued by the notion of a trial that might push him further, shifted the subject. He gestured to the young man beside him. "Well — anyway — my friend here, Old Ricky, wishes to make his way to [[Duchy of Brackenfall|Brackenfall]]. Can you give us directions? Which way is it?"

"Well... you see... it's actually in the opposite direction," the second watchman admitted. "West north-west from here. He'd have to depart [[Pithertara Province]], enter [[Saponia Province]], then head west to [[Athmolis Province]], keep heading west to [[Jukhara Province]], and then finally head north to enter Brackenfall."

Ironchest, not wanting to leave Ulriki stranded, asked whether there was a caravan he might catch. There would be one in the morning, they confirmed — though the caravan itself was the cheap part. The papers needed to cross the border from the Empire of Solara into Brackenfall would cost a good deal more.

Ellowen, catching the mention of her homeland, was suddenly listening very intently indeed.

Unaware that half the party sat directly behind him, Ironchest asked, "Do you know where I could get these papers for Old Ricky?"

"Here? Well, you see, it's going to be difficult to get them here. I reckon the keep may do them for you in its administrative arm, at dawn though. Getting in may also be a problem."

Ironchest turned the matter over privately — he had a military badge from the border guard near [[Campaign 2 - Shadows of the East/Player Notes/Settlements/Akrossa|Akrossa]], given to him under lucky circumstances when he'd crossed in from [[Duchy of Dornsia|Dornsia]] without papers of his own. "And where would this keep be?" he asked aloud.

"Just west of here. Not too far or difficult to get to — it's in the city, after all. Just expensive. It's currently closed too, mind, so that's another problem."

Ironchest sighed. "Hmmm. The keep is closed for the night, it is dusk after all. We'll sort out your passage to Brackenfall tomorrow. For now we should rest — it's been a long day." Ulriki went off toward his room, and from there out to tend to the Black Hare.

The fiery-haired minstrel from the market found her way to their table not long after, sitting herself down the moment she recognised the party. Nobody was quite sure yet whether she was following them on purpose. Yvaquis broke the silence first.

"What's your name, stranger?"

The woman brushed her hair from a freckled face. "Rhoda. [[Rhoda of the Shimmering Moontide - Player Notes|Rhoda of the Shimmering Moontide]]. I'm also called the Luminous Bard."

"How old are you?" Riley asked next.

"I'm twenty-five," she said, sounding faintly unsure of the answer, or perhaps of the question itself.

Ellowen's eyes lit with recognition. "[[The Shimmering Moontide]]! I've heard of them!" She pressed on, delighted. "The Luminous Bard, too? My fath— [[Alaric Aurelian Vale - Player Notes|the lord of the land I'm from]] has invited your travelling troupe to perform, several times."

Rhoda repeated herself for Riley's benefit, who was considerably less impressed. "Fair enough. I'm from the slums in Amphos. I have never heard of you, nor would I have ever had the chance to listen to your performances."

Yvaquis, ever polite, cut in. "Are you joining us? On our journey? It would be nice to have some music that isn't from Riley."

"Well — perhaps," Rhoda said, a touch flustered. "Maybe I can shuffle some things around. I am fully booked up, but I suppose I can do it!"

Ellowen asked what race she was, and got her answer: a merfairy, offspring of fae and merfolk both. Rare indeed, Ellowen told her — perhaps rare enough that she ought to be careful about who knew it. Privately, she resolved to study the woman's behaviour while she had the chance. Where was she from, exactly? The Shimmering Moonrise Reefs, Rhoda said, immediately south of here in the Moli Aeianao.

Yvaquis put it to the rest of the table plainly — should Rhoda join them? Everyone agreed. It was smart, having another set of hands.

Ellowen stepped outside not long after and found Ironchest tending to [[The Black Hare|his horse]] — a handsome bay, black-furred, a narrow white blaze down its face, feeding it grain to keep it from going hungry. Half a tonne of horse, easily, maybe more.

"That is a very lovely horse you've got there," Ellowen said.

"It's a very imposing horse," Ironchest agreed. "In fact, it was the strongest one of the group I could choose from. A former military war-horse for the empire, according to the stableman. I chose the most powerful horse for the most powerful man."

Bob, wandering out after her, had a question of his own. "Does your horse get big and beefy when you touch it? Like I do, when you touch me?"

Ironchest considered this seriously. "I've never done that, but I'm sure I could. I picked him up in a small village north of here, where a famous leatherworker named [[Basil (Leatherworker)|Basil]] produces his wares. The Black Hare carried me here, and is slowly becoming quite dear to me."

Ellowen finally steered toward the question she'd been circling all along. "I heard you mention Itrea and Brackenfall?"

"Well, Brackenfall is not my destination, it's—"

"It's _my_ destination!" Ulriki burst in, leaning out of his window above them, having clearly been listening. "I've been travelling from deep south to try and end up there! From [[Caliphate of Lqlima|Lqlima]], actually! I had felt like it was a good idea to travel north. I felt like something told me to."

Ellowen frowned — why would anyone travel from the southern deserts, from Lqlima of all places, all the way to Brackenfall? Things weren't good in Lqlima, certainly, but how much of that was simply Solari propaganda, worked so deep into her own thinking she couldn't tell the difference anymore? They were enemies, after all. "Why is it you need to go to Brackenfall?" she asked.

"It's a place I can be who I want to be," Ulriki said, and offered nothing more.

Ellowen turned back to Ironchest. "Are you journeying with him?"

"No, I'm sending him off. He catches a caravan in the morning," Ironchest said, a little sadly, though resigned to it. "I picked him up — same place I obtained my horse. In Akrossa. I've taken him under my wing for now, but we'll have to see where his journey takes him."

"Have you got passage to Itrea?" Ellowen pressed.

"No. All I know is that it's south of here, and the journey's by sea. I haven't a ship any more — mine is incapacitated."

"What happened?"

"I... well... long story short... I rammed it into the shore."

Ellowen didn't miss a beat. "Well, we have a ship, and we're headed to Itrea. We've also just had a lovely merfairy join us, and we have more room! Would you like to join us for the journey?"

"How much?" Ironchest asked, expecting to pay.

"So long as you help out and do your fair share on the ship, as well as supply us some food, I think we could call it even."

He accepted. He asked, too, about provisions for the horse — Ellowen admitted they might not have quite enough, but as long as he brought his own supply, the Black Hare could come along as well. Departure was set for sunrise, seven in the morning. Ellowen went in for a hug; Ironchest turned it into a firm handshake instead. As their hands met, every stone the party carried flared at once, as though recognising the deal now sealed — and in Bob's mind, the voice from the Soul Stone spoke again. _Keep power._

Ironchest checked the time — the seventh hour past noon — and frowned. He hadn't yet heard the screams he'd been braced for.

"What's wrong?" Ellowen asked, noticing his expression.

"The screams haven't happened yet," he said slowly. "In the forest, I touched the obelisk, and I had a vision. It said:

_one o'clock watch, two people_ _two o'clock watch, two people_ _three o'clock watch, two people_ _four o'clock watch, three people_ _five o'clock watch, not two but one person_ _six o'clock, scream._"

Ellowen went very still, recognising it instantly — the watch rota from the Zoraneset, that very morning. "So you had this vision... the other day, did you say?"

"No. Just yesterday."

"Do you happen to know if this was on a ship — this event?"

"No. I heard a wooden interior, and I could hear the crashing of waves."

The pieces fell together in her mind all at once. "This morning, you see — there was a scream on our ship, and our captain was murdered. What you just described was exactly what happened, with our watches, on that ship. You need to come along with us. We would very much like to discover more about what this power actually is."

Ironchest nodded slowly. "Well — I'm heading to Itrea, that is currently my destination. If that is the same, then so be it. I will join you."

"Well, Mr. Ironchest, we will see you early in the morning tomorrow, at the docks. After you've retrieved the grain for your stallion."

He pointed out, reasonably, that he had no way of picking their ship out from any other. Bob produced the painting of the Zoraneset he'd taken from the harbourmaster before they'd left Aklebe; Ironchest allowed that he could probably identify the dromon by the shields mounted along her sides. Ellowen took the opportunity to introduce him properly to the rest of the party, one by one.

It was while they stood there that a patch of greenish ooze, welling up from the cracks between the cobbles, drew itself together into a small gelatinous cube — no bigger than a wallet in any direction — settling on the street right beside Riley. It had an arrow for one arm, a dagger for the other, small eyes, and a mouth capable of forming expressions. It looked, more than anything, lost. Possibly alone. Possibly both.

Ellowen didn't hesitate for a second. She scooped it up with Mage Hand. "Oh! My! God! What is that adorable little thing!?"

The party gathered around as she held it aloft. "You're a cute little guy — can we be friends?" The cube gurgled, warmly, in what she chose to take as agreement.

Ironchest, deciding something that size posed little threat to anyone, excused himself for the night. Ellowen asked the cube if she might touch it, and it hardened its outer layer at once so as not to burn her. She asked its name; the gurgled reply, once she puzzled it out, was _[[Woubble]]_. She asked if he'd like to join them — he agreed — and settled him into a glass beaker tied to her belt, angled so he could see past the edge of her cloak and take in his surroundings. She swore, quite seriously, a pact of protection over him, and asked whether he had any ties here, any family. He had none. The party felt for him more than a little.

The group took a shared room at the tavern for the night. Riley settled at a table with his rum flask rather than patronising the bar directly. Ellowen went to arrange the room herself, and was told a single twin room was available for four silver.

"Ah! Perfect!" she said, a little too pleased.

The keep noticed. He raised the price to eight silver on the spot, explaining — not unreasonably — that her manner and her speech marked her as someone with money to spare, and richer guests paid more. Ellowen wasn't having it. She threatened to find lodging elsewhere and began to walk. The bartender caved, settling for three silver, and handed over the key with poor grace.

Riley, meanwhile, had drifted back to the watchmen's table, still drinking, hood up, and dropped himself into the seat across from them.

"I heard you were talking about tabaxi earlier..." he said, in his own low, masculine voice.

The watchmen exchanged a glance. "That's an odd voice for that face," one said.

"You were talking about tabaxi, yeah? I heard you talk about it — I was over there!" He gestured, sloppily, at the table directly behind them, spilling rum from the flask still clutched loosely in his hand.

"Woah! Mind your drink — but yes, we were. We do know about the tabaxi, the cat-folk," the first watchman said, willing enough to indulge him.

"Don't tell me to mind my drink!" Riley snapped.

"Mind. Your. Drink." The watchman's voice hardened — a real warning, to anyone sober enough to catch it.

Ellowen swept in before it could go further. "Apologies for my drunk friend here. He's going through a rough time."

"I'm not drunk!" Riley slurred, entirely drunk.

"He has been going through a very rough time," Ellowen said firmly, to the watchmen — then, sharper, to Riley, "Have you not?!" She turned back to the table. "What he wants to know is, what is it you know about the tabaxi race?"

The watchmen had calmed by now. "Not too much," the second said. "Only really what we read recently, with that scrawny teenager, and some things from personal experience."

"Would you care to indulge us, sirs?" Ellowen asked.

"Well, I know there's this one really problem tabaxi in the capital," the first offered. "Keeps stealing from us, the royals, the rich. Real pain in the arse."

"Oh! Well, that is quite unfortunate..." Ellowen said, already certain who they meant.

"The poor love that tabaxi — but it just goes about causing so many problems. It's a nightmare."

"What is it that this person has been doing?"

"Just general thievery, a bit of threat, broke into the palace archive, tried to murder somebody higher up — treason. Y'know, they probably think of themself as some kind of vigilante, folk hero. We captured them once, but got an order from the Donpotes to release them, for some odd reason. To think we had the problem in our hands, and it just got away!"

Riley's temper broke. He drained his flask and hurled it to the ground with a loud clang. "That person sounds like a fucking hero to me!" he snarled, finger jabbed at the watchmen.

Ellowen was already moving, hand pressed to his chest. "I think you need some rest, friend." Riley dropped instantly into an enforced sleep.

The watchmen carried on, unbothered, now that Ellowen had the floor. "The head of military, the Donpotes da Tagaminase, is also a tabaxi — he must've been one of the two on the census."

"Oh, do you mean General...?" Ellowen ventured, feeling out how much they knew.

"Yes! Zenshoran Hagashar, head of the military. Comes from a different country, but befriended the king quickly enough. Came over maybe twenty-odd years back."

"And who's the other tabaxi? Do you know her?"

"Ah, well, she's a right pain!"

"_She?_" Ellowen asked, wanting it confirmed.

"Yes, she. Goes around the palace causing so many problems. You can guarantee if there's trouble, she and the prince are there sticking their noses in. She's really close to him!"

Ellowen breathed out, slow and heavy. She already knew exactly who this was. "Is she an apothecary, by any chance?"

"I don't know! All I know is she causes problems!"

"Who is this tabaxi related to?" Ellowen pushed on.

"Related to? No one that I know of. I'm not high up. I'm just a soldier — I just get posted places and hear gossip."

"Have you heard much of Brackenfall?" Ellowen tried, changing tack, gathering anything she could.

"I know where it is. Haven't heard much of it recently."

"No news? I heard the prince of Amphos is betrothed to a Brackenfallian elite."

"Ah, yes! The lady. The Lady Ellowen. I'm not of House Aurelian Vale myself, but their holiday and mercenary keep in Amphos — it's an asset to the capital, and beautiful besides. Nothing else new though. Next stop for me is Itrea, actually — where the prince is headed."

"Why is the prince going there?"

"Not in my detail. I believe it's just protection, on a diplomatic mission."

"What is the prince like?"

"He's quite nice, but incredibly intrusive. Tends to find problems, or find himself _in_ a problem, then get that tabaxi to solve it for him. Absolute nightmare. Causes even more problems, if you ask me."

Ellowen thanked them, wished them a good night, and hauled the unconscious Riley up the stairs with Mage Hand — making sure, with what could only be called deliberate malice, that he collided with every stair, snag, and bump along the way. She stopped briefly back downstairs to place an advance order for breakfast, two silver well spent.

Upstairs, with Bob, Yvaquis, Rhoda, and Riley all sprawled across the room in various states of sleep, Ellowen sat at the desk, opened her journal, and began recording the day. When she'd finished, she settled in to meditate — and somewhere in her sleep, without meaning to, cast Ray of Frost squarely at Riley, freezing a chunk of cheese and bread neatly to his cheek.
